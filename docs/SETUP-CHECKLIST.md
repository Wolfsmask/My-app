# Business Setup Checklist

The website is done. These are the real-world things that have to exist before money can
change hands, written for a founder who is under 18.

**None of this is legal or tax advice.** Go through it with a parent or guardian, and
verify anything that involves money or signatures.

---

## 1. Things that block you right now

| Blocker | Why it blocks you | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **No way to accept payment** | Stripe, PayPal, Square and Wise all require account holders to be 18+ | A parent opens the account, or an LLC is formed with a parent as organizer and the account opens under the business |
| **Contracts are voidable** | In the US, a contract signed by a minor can generally be cancelled by the minor. Many businesses won't sign because of it | A parent or guardian co-signs as the responsible party on every client agreement |
| **No postal address** | Commercial email legally requires a real physical mailing address in every message (CAN-SPAM) | A parent rents a USPS PO Box (~$25–40 per 3 months) or a virtual mailbox (~$10–15/month) |
| **No business entity** | Not required to start, but protects personal assets and makes you look real | Missouri LLC, ~$50 online through the Secretary of State. A parent needs to organize it |

**Do not use your home address anywhere public.** Not in the footer, not in email. You are
going to be emailing hundreds of strangers. A PO Box costs about $10/month and removes
that problem permanently.

## 2. What is already solved

- **Phone number** — you don't need one. The site now says email and video call only, which
  is normal for web work. If you want one later, Google Voice gives a free US number.
- **Email** — `mbonyxstudios@gmail.com` is live across the site. It's fine to start with. If
  you buy `mbonyx.com` later (~$12/year), Cloudflare will forward `hello@mbonyx.com` to your
  Gmail for free, which reads more professional on invoices.
- **Location** — the site now says "Liberty, Missouri — working with clients anywhere in
  the US." Being local is a trust signal; being remote-capable is a reach signal. You get both.

## 3. About your age

**Don't lie about it. Don't lead with it.**

- The site says "I", never "we". There is no fake team, no fake founding year, no fake
  client logos. Everything on it is true.
- If a client asks how old you are, tell them. Lying and getting caught mid-project costs
  you the client and the referral.
- Don't volunteer it in a cold email either. The first email should be about *their*
  website, not about you. Age comes up on a call, if at all, and by then they have already
  seen work they liked.
- Some clients will pass because of it. More than you'd expect won't care, because what
  they're buying is the finished site — and they can see the finished site.

A parent being the one who signs and invoices solves most of the practical hesitation.

---

## 4. Safety rules

Non-negotiable, because you'll be contacting strangers:

- **Never meet a client alone.** Video calls only, or a parent present.
- **Never publish your home address**, school, or daily schedule.
- **A parent should be on the account** that receives money and on the email if possible.
- **If a "client" pushes to meet in person, wants personal details, or the conversation
  stops being about the website — stop and tell a parent.** Legitimate business owners
  will happily do everything over email.

## 5. The referral program

Your dad's idea, written up as real terms. It's now live on the site under
[Referrals](/#referrals).

### How it works

1. Someone tells a business about you
2. That business names them in the "who referred you" field when they inquire
3. When that project finishes and final payment clears, the referrer gets **15%**

| Package | Client pays | Referrer gets |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | $900 | $135 |
| Business | $1,800 | $270 |
| Signature | $3,200 | $480 |

### The one change I made to the idea

Your dad described it as chaining — A refers B, B refers C, and so on.

I built it as **single tier**: you earn on the people *you* refer directly. You do **not**
earn on the people they later refer.

Why that matters:

- **Single tier is a referral program.** Totally standard, totally legal, every agency
  runs one.
- **Multi-tier, where you earn off other people's referrals, starts to look like multi-level
  marketing.** That carries real regulatory attention, it's hard to track, and — more
  practically — business owners recognise the shape of it and it damages your credibility.
  "Recruit people under you" is a very different pitch from "thanks for the introduction."

You lose almost nothing. The people actually referring you are the direct ones anyway, and
a chain that pays three levels deep on a $1,800 project is pennies at the bottom.

**The rest of his idea is exactly right** — especially paying only on completed
transactions. That protects you from paying out on deals that fall through, which is the
mistake most people make.

### Practical notes

- **Put it in writing.** One page: rate, when it's paid, that it's one level, and that
  nothing is owed if the project doesn't complete. Have a parent look at it.
- **Confirm the referral with the referrer** before starting, so nobody claims credit later.
- **Track it** — a spreadsheet is fine: who referred, who bought, amount, paid yes/no.
- **Tax:** if you pay any one person $600 or more in a calendar year, US rules generally
  require issuing them a 1099-NEC. Worth knowing before it happens, not after.
- **15% is deliberately generous.** You have no ad budget and no track record. Paying $270
  for a client who pays you $1,800 is a fantastic trade, and it costs you nothing until
  after you've already been paid.

## 6. Order to do things in

1. **Talk to your dad about the payment account.** Everything else is blocked on this — you
   cannot get paid without it. Do this first.
2. **PO Box.** Required before any cold emailing. Can happen in parallel.
3. **Write the referral agreement.** One page. You need it before the first referral, not after.
4. **Get the first client without cold email.** See below. Cold email is Phase 3, not Phase 1.
5. **LLC** — only once you have a paying client or two. Don't spend $50 on paperwork before
   you've made a dollar.

---

## 7. Your first clients will not come from the app

This matters. The outreach app is a Phase 3+ tool. It needs a warmed-up domain, a PO Box,
and a month of setup before it sends anything.

Meanwhile, you already have the two things that get a first client:

- **Five finished demo sites**, which is more portfolio than most beginners have
- **A referral program** that pays people to talk about you

Faster paths to client #1:

| Path | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Businesses your family already uses | Your dentist, your barber, your dad's coworkers. Warm intro, no cold email needed, no PO Box required |
| Your dad's network | Adults who own businesses know other adults who own businesses. This is what the referral program is for |
| Liberty / Kansas City small businesses, in person | Walk in with the demo on a laptop. A 14-year-old who built five working websites is memorable in a way an email is not |
| Local chamber of commerce or Facebook business groups | Free, and people there are actively looking for services |

**Get two paying clients this way first.** Then you have real testimonials, real screenshots
of real work, and proof the pricing holds — which makes the cold email dramatically more
likely to work when you do turn it on.
